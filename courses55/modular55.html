Modular 55: Modular Forms and Automorphic Forms

Classical modular forms, then Maass forms, trace formulas and families of LLL-functions

Two terms in the Math 55 rhythm. The first is classical modular forms, arithmetic and computational; the second is the analytic theory that analytic number theorists use every day: Maass forms, the spectral decomposition, the Petersson and Kuznetsov formulas, and families of LLL-functions.

Overview

What the course is

The first term rests on Milne's Modular Functions and Modular Forms, recommended reading for the Cambridge Part III course, and on Stein's Modular Forms, a Computational Approach, whose free online version shows how to compute every space of forms in SageMath. The second term uses lecture notes from courses by Sarnak, Goldfeld's school and others on the spectral theory of automorphic forms.

Assessment

Problem sets, exams and project

Weekly set: 8–10 problems, one of them computational in the first term.

  • Problem sources: the exercises in Bruin–Dahmen and Milne, the exercises at the end of each chapter of Stein, and the exercises in Chatzakos' notes.
  • Exams: two and a half hours, closed book; in 55b include one problem that asks for the main term of a spectral sum.

Project

A 10–15 page exposition of one of the following:

  1. A full proof of the Petersson formula with an application to Fourier coefficients of cusp forms.
  2. The Kuznetsov formula and its application to sums of Kloosterman sums.
  3. A computational study of the Sato–Tate distribution for a newform of small level.
