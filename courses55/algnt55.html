AlgNT 55: Algebraic Number Theory and Class Field Theory

Number fields, local fields, adeles, class field theory and Tate's thesis

Two terms in the Math 55 rhythm covering the algebraic number theory that every number theorist is examined on: the first term is a standard first graduate course (as in MIT 18.785), the second is class field theory and Tate's thesis.

Overview

What the course is

The first term follows the structure of Andrew Sutherland's MIT course 18.785 and Milne's Algebraic Number Theory notes, both free. The second term uses Milne's Class Field Theory for the algebraic side and Sutherland's last lectures on adeles and ideles for the bridge to Tate's thesis and Hecke LLL-functions, which is where this course meets analytic number theory.

Galois theory is assumed; Milne's Fields and Galois Theory is a free refresher.

Assessment

Problem sets, exams and project

Weekly set: 8–10 problems, one of them a computation in PARI/GP or SageMath (both free).

  • Problem sources: the problem sets of MIT 18.785 and the exercises at the end of each chapter of Milne's two sets of notes.
  • Exams: two and a half hours, closed book: one full computation (for example a class group with proof), two theorem statements with one proof, and one conceptual question.

Project

A 10–15 page exposition of one of the following:

  1. A complete proof of Chebotarev's density theorem from class field theory.
  2. Tate's thesis written out for Q\mathbb{Q}Q, ending with the functional equation of Dirichlet LLL-functions.
  3. Primes of the form x2+ny2x^2+ny^2x2+ny2 through the Hilbert and ring class fields, with numerical tables.
