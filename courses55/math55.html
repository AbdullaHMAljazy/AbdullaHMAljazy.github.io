Overview

What the course is

Math 55 is Harvard's honours first-year course, created by Lynn Loomis and Shlomo Sternberg. Its official names today are Studies in Algebra and Group Theory (55a) and Studies in Real and Complex Analysis (55b). What sets it apart is not the list of topics, which is close to Math 25, but the level of abstraction and the pace.

Harvard's texts are Axler and Artin for 55a, and Munkres and Ahlfors (with Rudin) for 55b. Axler is legally free; the other three are not, so the plan below is built on the free notes of McMullen, Elkies, Hatcher, Lebl and Etingof. The matching chapter of the official book is given for readers who can borrow it.

TermWhat it covers
55a — AlgebraGroups; abstract linear algebra up to Jordan form and the spectral theorems; multilinear algebra (tensor products, exterior algebra, determinants); modules and finitely generated abelian groups; group actions and the Sylow theorems; representations and characters of finite groups.
55b — Analysis and topologyPoint-set topology; the fundamental group, covering spaces and van Kampen; real analysis (Arzelà–Ascoli, Stone–Weierstrass, Fourier series, inverse and implicit function theorems, differential forms and Stokes); complex analysis at the level of Ahlfors, up to Γ\GammaΓ, ζ\zetaζ and the Weierstrass ℘\wp℘-function.

Method

How to work

  1. Rhythm: three lectures a week (reading or video) for 13 weeks per term.
  2. Before each lecture: read the assigned section and rebuild every main proof from memory before moving on.
  3. Problem sets: 8–10 problems a week from the sources below, written up in full in LaTeX. Do not look at posted solutions until you have worked on a problem for 48 hours.
  4. Exams: two midterms and a final per term (weeks 5, 11 and 13 in 55a; weeks 4, 9 and 13 in 55b), timed and closed-book.
  5. Checking: show your solutions to a classmate or a teacher. Formalising one result a week in Lean 4 with Mathlib is a good self-check: groups, vector spaces, compactness and connectedness are all there.

Do 55a before 55b: the algebra of 55a (inner product spaces, exterior algebra, groups) is used in differential forms and the fundamental group.

Assessment

Problem sets, exams and project

The current Math 55 problem sets live inside Harvard's Canvas, but the problem sets of earlier years are public.

  • First choice (original 55 level): the problem sets on Elkies' pages for 55a (2016) and 55b (2017–18), and McMullen's 55a course page (2009).
  • Algebra: Axler's exercises, the problem sets of MIT 18.702 (Artin), and the problems in Etingof's book.
  • Topology: Hatcher's chapter 1 exercises and his additional exercises, and MIT 18.901 problem sets.
  • Analysis: Lebl's exercises and the problem sets and exams of MIT 18.100A.
  • Exams: build each midterm from 6–8 problems you have not solved: two definitions, two theorem statements with one proof, and computations such as a character table or a residue integral, in two and a half hours.

For any algebra topic that resists you, Keith Conrad's expository notes are the best second reference.

Shortcut

A compressed path for graduate students (about 16 weeks)

If you already hold a degree in mathematics, you do not need the same pace everywhere. Go deep where the course adds most for later work in number theory and geometry:

  1. Full depth, a problem set every week: 55a weeks 5–8 (Jordan form, spectral theorems, tensor and exterior algebra) and 12–13 (representations). These underlie derived categories and modular forms.
  2. Full depth: 55b weeks 5–6 (π1\pi_1π1​ and coverings) and 11–13 (complex analysis at the level of Ahlfors).
  3. Fast review (midterms only): 55a weeks 1–4 and 9–11, 55b weeks 1–4 and 7–10. If a problem stops you, go back and do that week in full.
