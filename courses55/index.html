55 Courses

Two-term self-study courses in the format of Harvard's Math 55, built entirely from free resources

Each course takes the structure that makes Harvard's Math 55 work, a weekly problem set that carries most of the weight, two midterms and a final each term, and a fast, steady pace, and applies it to a different area of mathematics. Every week lists the topics, a free reading and, where useful, a video or a computation.

The format

What every 55 course has in common

  1. Two terms of 13 weeks, about three lectures' worth of reading or video a week.
  2. A weekly problem set of 8–10 problems, written up in full in LaTeX. In the Harvard course it carries about two thirds of the grade; here it is the course itself.
  3. Two midterms and a final each term, timed and closed-book, built from problems you have not seen.
  4. Only free resources: authors' own notes and books, open courseware, arXiv. Paid textbooks are named only for comparison.
  5. A project at the end of the advanced courses: a 10–20 page exposition or research draft.

Order

Suggested order and time

Math 55 (or an equivalent undergraduate education) comes first. After that the courses split into two lines that can run in parallel: an analytic line (ANT 55 with Prob 55) and an arithmetic–geometric line (AlgNT 55, then Modular 55; AG 55 independently).

At about 20 hours a week, one course takes half a year per term. Two courses in parallel need 35–40 hours a week. Nobody needs all six before starting research: learn a tool when a problem calls for it.
